# 02_BUSINESS_RULES.md

**Project Name:** Embroidery Business Management System (EBMS)

**Developer Kit Version:** 1.0

**Document Version:** 1.0

**Status:** Approved

**Document Type:** Business Rules

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the official business rules that govern how the software behaves.

Business rules are **non-negotiable**. Every API, database model, validation rule, and user interface must follow these rules.

If an implementation conflicts with this document, the implementation must be corrected.

---

# 2. General Principles

The system must always:

* Preserve business data.
* Prevent accidental mistakes.
* Maintain historical records.
* Keep financial records accurate.
* Avoid duplicate information.
* Prefer warnings over silent failures.

---

# 3. Customer Rules

### BR-001

Every Job must belong to exactly one Customer.

A Job cannot exist without a Customer.

---

### BR-002

Customer Name is mandatory.

Phone Number is optional.

---

### BR-003

Customers cannot be permanently deleted if they have business history.

Instead, they must be archived using Soft Delete.

---

### BR-004

Archived customers remain searchable in reports and historical records.

---

### BR-005

Duplicate customers should be prevented whenever possible.

The system should warn the user before creating another customer with the same name and phone number.

---

# 4. Job Rules

### BR-101

Every Job belongs to exactly one Customer.

---

### BR-102

Every Job contains one or more Job Items.

A Job without Job Items is invalid.

---

### BR-103

Every Job receives a unique Job Number generated by the system.

Format: JOB-{YYYY}-{NNNNNN}

* YYYY — 4-digit year the Job was created.
* NNNNNN — 6-digit zero-padded sequential counter.
* The counter resets to 000001 on January 1st of each year.
* Job Numbers are unique within a calendar year and must never be reused.

Example:

JOB-2026-000125

---

### BR-104

Job Status changes follow this sequence:

Draft

↓

In Progress

↓

Completed

↓

Delivered

Cancelled is allowed from any unfinished state.

---

### BR-105

Delivered Jobs should never return to Draft.

---

### BR-106

Jobs with generated invoices should not allow structural changes without following the approved correction process.

---

# 5. Job Item Rules

### BR-201

Each Job Item represents one embroidery operation.

Examples:

* Collar
* Pocket
* Sleeve
* Cap

---

### BR-202

Each Job Item stores:

* Quantity
* Rate
* Position
* Thread Color
* Design
* Dimensions
* Remarks

---

### BR-203

Quantity must always be greater than zero.

---

### BR-204

Rate cannot be negative.

---

### BR-205

Each Job Item has its own production status.

---

# 6. Design Rules

### BR-301

Designs may be reused across multiple Jobs.

---

### BR-302

Deleting a Design must not remove historical references.

Instead, archive it.

---

### BR-303

Multiple files may be attached to a Design.

---

# 7. Invoice Rules

### BR-401

Invoices are generated only from completed Job Items.

---

### BR-402

An Invoice belongs to one Customer.

---

### BR-403

An Invoice contains one or more Invoice Items.

---

### BR-404

Invoice Number must be unique.

Format: INV-{YYYY}-{NNNNNN}

* YYYY — 4-digit year the Invoice was created.
* NNNNNN — 6-digit zero-padded sequential counter.
* The counter resets to 000001 on January 1st of each year.
* Invoice Numbers are unique within a calendar year and must never be reused.
* A cancelled Invoice Number is retired and may not be reassigned.

Example:

INV-2026-000021

---

### BR-405

Paid Invoices must not be edited directly.

Corrections should be handled through an approved adjustment workflow in future versions.

---

### BR-406

Invoice totals are calculated by the system.

Users should not manually enter the total amount.

---

### BR-407

An Invoice may carry an optional Discount.

When a Discount is applied, the Discount Type must be specified:

* Percentage — a value between 0 and 100 applied to the Invoice subtotal.
* Fixed Amount — a fixed monetary value deducted from the subtotal.

Calculation:

* Percentage: Discount Amount = ROUND(Subtotal × Discount Value / 100, 2)
* Fixed Amount: Discount Amount = Discount Value (must not exceed the Subtotal)

Grand Total = Subtotal − Discount Amount

Grand Total must always be greater than zero after discount is applied.

The calculated Discount Amount is stored permanently as part of the Invoice financial snapshot and must never be recalculated after the Invoice is issued.

# 8. Payment Rules

### BR-501

A Payment belongs to one Customer.

---

### BR-502

One Payment may settle one or multiple Invoices.

---

### BR-503

One Invoice may receive multiple Payments until fully paid.

---

### BR-504

The system must support partial payments.

---

### BR-505

The system must never allow allocated payment amounts to exceed the invoice balance.

---

### BR-506

Every Payment receives a unique Payment Number.

Format: PAY-{YYYY}-{NNNNNN}

* YYYY — 4-digit year the Payment was created.
* NNNNNN — 6-digit zero-padded sequential counter.
* The counter resets to 000001 on January 1st of each year.
* Payment Numbers are unique within a calendar year and must never be reused.

Example:

PAY-2026-000078

---

### BR-507

Version 1 supports the following Payment Methods:

* Cash
* UPI
* Bank Transfer
* Cheque

Payment Method is mandatory for every Payment record.

No other payment method values are valid in Version 1.

---

# 9. Attachment Rules

### BR-601

Attachments may belong to:

* Customer
* Job
* Job Item
* Design
* Invoice

Payments do not support attachments in Version 1.

---

### BR-602

Deleting an attachment must not affect the business record.

# 11. Dashboard Rules

The Dashboard should answer:

"What requires attention today?"

It should prioritize:

* Pending Jobs
* Pending Payments
* Recently Completed Jobs
* Recent Activity

The dashboard should not become a statistics page.

# 12. Data Integrity Rules

### BR-801

All financial operations must use database transactions.

---

### BR-802

Business records must never be permanently deleted through normal operations.

Three categories govern record lifecycle:

**Category 1 — Operational Records (use Archive)**

The following entities are archived using a `deleted_at` timestamp:

* Customers
* Designs
* Jobs
* Job Items
* Attachments

The user-facing action is called "Archive". The word "Delete" must not appear in user interfaces or user-facing documentation for these records.

Archived records are excluded from normal queries but remain permanently accessible in historical and audit contexts.

**Category 2 — Financial Records (status transitions only, never archived or deleted)**

The following entities use status transitions and are never archived:

* Invoices (DRAFT → ISSUED → PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID / CANCELLED)
* Invoice Items (immutable after Invoice issuance)
* Payments (DRAFT → CONFIRMED)
* Payment Allocations (immutable after Payment confirmation)

A CANCELLED Invoice remains in the system with status CANCELLED. The Invoice Number is retired and must never be reused within the same calendar year.

**Category 3 — System Records (never deleted)**

* Audit Logs are append-only and must never be deleted.
* Settings are updated in place and never deleted.

---

### BR-803

Every business record stores:

* Created At
* Updated At

Operational records (Category 1) additionally store:

* Deleted At (NULL when active; populated when Archived)

Financial records (Category 2) do not use a Deleted At field. Their state is represented exclusively through status fields.

---

### BR-804

Primary keys use UUID.

Public-facing identifiers use business-friendly numbers.

---

# 13. Security Rules

* Never trust client input.
* Validate all requests on the server.
* Sanitize user input.
* Protect uploaded files.
* Restrict sensitive operations.

---

# 14. Error Handling Rules

Business errors must be understandable.

Good:

"Invoice has already been paid."

Bad:

"500 Internal Server Error"

Users should always understand what happened and what they can do next.

---

# 15. Future Expansion Rules

Future features must not require redesigning the existing database.

New functionality should extend the current architecture rather than replacing it.

Version 1 establishes the foundation for future growth.

---

# 16. Summary

These business rules define how the software behaves.

Every future implementation—whether written by a human developer or generated by AI—must comply with these rules unless an officially approved revision updates this document.
